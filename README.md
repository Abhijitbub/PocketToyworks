# Pocket Toyworks prototype

A portrait, offline fitting puzzle built from the Pocket Toyworks GDD 1.3. A guided tutorial and ten authored orders turn pieces into Pip, an original miniature robot. The starter uses **Unity 6000.3.23f1 / URP 17.3.0**; keep that Editor version.

Updated build: `Builds/Android/PocketToyworks-0.5.0.apk` (version 0.5.0 / Android code 8, ARM64, Android 8+). The canonical development APK and Windows player remain under `Builds/Android` and `Builds/Windows`. See `Documentation/UPDATE-0.5.0.md` and `Verification` for the revision evidence.

Version 0.5.0 makes timed play the only main-level flow. Play, Continue, replay and Next Level all use the countdown. Undo is free and never restores time; restarting a started attempt or timing out costs one life. Home, backgrounding, settings and the tutorial save and pause without a charge. Switching levels preserves separate boards and clocks. Only the guided tutorial remains untimed and exempt from lives. The previous lane-title, typography, texture and corner repairs remain, with portrait 16:9 and 18:9 review.

## Open and play

1. Open this folder with Unity 6000.3.23f1.
2. Open `Assets/PocketToyworks/Generated/Workshop.unity` and press Play. Use a portrait Game view, ideally 720 × 1280 or 360 × 800.
3. Fresh installs begin with a four-step tutorial for dragging, parking and rotation. Tap **Learn** to replay it; Continue restores your interrupted order. Select a lane's front piece, then tap a mould cell. The tapped cell anchors the upper-left of the normalized shape. Rotate is explicit and remembered per piece when you drag again, switch selection or park it. Dragging uses the exact footprint above the pointer/finger.
4. Tap an empty reserve while a lane front is selected to park it. Tap a saved piece to select it. Tap the lower preview region of a lane to inspect all remaining pieces.
5. Undo and Hint are free. Restart costs one life after the clock starts, with confirmation, even after undoing every move. Undo never restores time. Home always saves and pauses without losing a life. Settings include sound, music, haptics and reduced motion. Hints provide a verified route or explain how many undos recover one. Final results cannot be reopened through undo.
6. Home shows the ten-level trail, lives, coins and your profile. Complete levels to unlock the next, earn 50 coins on each first clear, and add toys to the shelf. A level card has one Play or Continue button and its time limit: three minutes for levels 1–5 and four for levels 6–10. Lives regenerate every 30 minutes while away; claim 75 coins once every 24 hours. The Workshop uses earned coins for refills, boosters and frames. Zero lives block new attempts but allow the tutorial and resuming an unfinished saved attempt. There are no real-money purchases or ads.
7. Edit your profile name, select one of eight original toy characters and choose a frame. Time boosts unlock at level 4; verified auto-fit unlocks at level 7. The previous rotation fixes and tutorial remain in this update.

Normal progression unlocks levels sequentially; completed levels remain replayable. Existing saves keep their puzzle and completed orders. The two separate single/dual comparison IDs remain available as validated content for development studies.

## Regenerate assets and content

Choose **Pocket Toyworks → Regenerate prototype assets**. The command owns only `Assets/PocketToyworks/Generated`, its level JSON files, and the prototype build entry. It preserves the starter SampleScene. It creates the robot prefab, rounded mesh, five plastic materials, tuning asset, and fully wired game scene. It is repeatable; edit tuning through `WorkshopTuning.asset`. Intentional edits to generated meshes/materials/content should also be made in the authoring code before regenerating.

The JSON files under `Assets/PocketToyworks/Resources/Levels` contain stable piece IDs, cells, lane order, masks, teaching text, and complete solution traces. `Editor/ContentAuthoring.cs` holds the readable letter tilings. Each letter is one connected piece; `.` is blocked. No runtime randomness is used.

The original conceptual screens are `Documentation/Mockups/{gameplay,reveal,collection}.svg`. Regenerate them with `Tools/CreateMockups.ps1`. These show the early direction. Current gameplay and the working shelf are captured under `Verification/Screenshots/v0.5.0`. Original glossy UI artwork is generated by `Tools/CreateUIAssets.cjs`; SVG sources live under `Documentation/VisualSources`. See `Documentation/ASSETS.md` for the display-font license.

## Test and build

- **Pocket Toyworks → Validate all content** replays every trace against the actual model, checks legal moves and conservation, and writes `Verification/content-validation.txt`.
- Open **Window → General → Test Runner**, choose **EditMode**, and run the PocketToyworks tests.
- **Pocket Toyworks → Build Android development APK** builds an ARM64 IL2CPP development APK to `Builds/Android/PocketToyworks-development.apk`. Requires the Editor's Android SDK, NDK and OpenJDK modules. Minimum Android API is 26. No ad SDK or account is required.
- A Windows verification player can be built with `-buildTarget Win64 -executeMethod PocketToyworks.Editor.WorkshopSetup.BuildWindows` in batch mode. For a command-line Android build, use `-buildTarget Android -executeMethod PocketToyworks.Editor.WorkshopSetup.BuildAndroid`. Explicit target selection is required before building so Unity prepares the correct URP resources.
- Launch that development player with `--verify-workshop` to run real Input System mouse events through the scene UI and capture single, dual, narrow portrait and result screenshots. Run it with this project as the working directory. It writes under `Verification`, uses a separate test save, and exits when finished.

Example batch test invocation in PowerShell (close this project in the Editor first, or use a separate validation copy):

```powershell
& 'C:\Program Files\Unity\Hub\Editor\6000.3.23f1\Editor\Unity.exe' -batchmode -nographics -projectPath $PWD -runTests -testPlatform EditMode -testResults "$PWD/Verification/editmode-results.xml" -logFile "$PWD/Verification/unity-tests.log"
```

## Architecture and local data

`Core/Puzzle.cs` is a Unity-independent C# assembly: immutable-on-command snapshots, normalized 90° rotations, legal moves, exact jams, conservation validation, trace replay and bounded search. `Runtime/WorkshopView.cs` owns presentation/input and dispatches commands. `Services.cs` separates content, saving, telemetry and future ads. Search runs on a worker with a 100 ms/15,000-node budget and cancellation when the board changes. Timeout is reported as unknown; baseline trace snapshots provide a guaranteed recovery route.

Saves and event JSONL live in `Application.persistentDataPath/PocketToyworksPrototype`. The save envelope retains schema/content version 1, a checksum, flushed temporary file and atomic replacement with `.previous` recovery. Loading verifies every historical state transition. Unknown envelope versions are rejected. Progression migration version 2 retains settings, undo history, completed levels, profiles, balances and rewards. Untimed 0.4.0 attempts receive a full timer once; timed attempts retain their remaining clock. When paused modes share a level ID, migration prefers the timed attempt. Suspended tutorial puzzles follow the same conversion. Permanent flags and first-clear rewards are never duplicated by undo/replay. Paint, palettes and a production save schema remain deferred.

Telemetry is local only: session ID, content/level ID, action count, active play time and event details. It does not transmit or collect personal information. Pause, menus, and completed boards do not count as active puzzle time. There is no backend or live advertising. `IAds` has a no-op provider; future advertising constraints are recorded in the GDD extraction and decision log.

## Manual playtest

- Start offline and finish levels 1–10 using both tap and drag. Explain the queue fronts and reserves without coaching.
- Rotate near all four edges. Drop over blocked/occupied cells and outside the board; confirm no state changes.
- Park both reserves, inspect a full queue, use a reserve in either mould, and exhaust a lane.
- Complete an intermediate mould, undo its reveal, and verify pieces, reserve, queue heads and replacement are restored. A final result must reject undo and retain completion.
- Make a bad but still legal choice; ask for a hint. Confirm timeout is not called impossible and an undo route is explained when needed.
- Background immediately after the final fit; reopen and check exact resume and no duplicate completion flag.
- Replay a completed order. Toggle each setting, restart the app, and check that settings persist.
- Edit the name using the Android keyboard, change character/frame, and reopen the app. Verify the profile, coins, gifts and booster stock persist.
- Confirm and cancel restart, including after undoing all moves; check one life is charged only on confirmation. Verify Home/background/tutorial pauses, switching saved levels, time boosts, timeout/retry and no duplicate life loss. At zero lives, block new attempts and allow existing saved attempts, tutorial and Workshop access. Verify Next Level and replay both use the timer.
- Check the offline life countdown, daily-gift cooldown, zero-life screen, earned-coin refill and locked/unlocked level trail.
- Inspect 360 × 640 and 360 × 800, safe-area devices and one-handed touch. Verify that the finger does not obscure the placement footprint.
- Test on a physical baseline Android device with 4 GB RAM: startup, sustained frame time, audio, 12 ms haptics, thermal behavior and pause/resume. Desktop captures do not establish mobile performance or usability.

See `Documentation/PROGRESS.md` and `Verification` for measured results and remaining work. Builds, the validation copy, Unity caches and logs are ignored by version control. Nothing is published or uploaded.
